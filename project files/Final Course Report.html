Data-Driven Surrogate for the Stress Response of AlSi10Mg

Multiscale Modelling of an Additively Manufactured Wing-Rib Bracket — ML Component
Team 8
October 2026
Abstract We trained a small neural-network surrogate that predicts the von Mises stress response Svm of AlSi10Mg along the operational strain E11, given temperature, strain rate, density and shear modulus. The final predictor averages five 5–16–16–1 tanh networks (385 parameters each, 1,925 in total). On a fixed split of 37 held-out simulations, the mean per-curve RMSE is 0.0591 (worst 0.1133, case 45). Performance is much weaker when whole temperature/rate conditions are held out (mean 6.79), so the model closely reproduces the 37 related test curves, but its reliability at other input combinations, even inside the numerical ranges, is not established. Units are inferred, the held-out set is not fully blind, and the link from material porosity to the bracket's critical flaw length CL has not been established. These limits are stated throughout.

1. Introduction and Scope

The wider project studies how porosity in an additively manufactured AlSi10Mg wing-rib bracket affects its structural integrity, expressed as the critical flaw length CL: the largest defect the bracket can carry at its design stress before fracture. This report covers only the machine-learning component: a fast surrogate for the material's simulated stress response, intended to replace repeated material simulations inside a larger multiscale workflow. The structural (ANSYS) analysis is reported separately and is not covered here. Throughout, CL means critical flaw length, not the aerodynamic coefficient of lift; the material data contains no flaw sizes or fracture-toughness values, so this report makes no CL prediction.

2. Data

The professor's design matrix planned 108 simulations; 73 were available, containing 2,197,000 raw history rows. Temperature coverage is uneven: 27 cases at 30, 27 at 100, 19 at 200 and none at 300 (units inferred as °C). Each case also varies strain rate (10−5 to 10−3, inferred s−1), density (2488.05 to 2700) and shear modulus G (23313.95 to 25300).

The target is Svm along operational E11, not SCAU11. Each history was interpolated onto a common grid of 120 E11 values from 0.0002 to 0.0999, so long, slow-rate runs do not dominate by row count. The histories do not show a conventional zero-stress elastic loading segment, so the surrogate should not be read as a constitutive law.

3. Method

3.1 Split and Leakage Control

We used one fixed random split by simulation (seed 42): 36 training cases and 37 test cases, following the professor's requested 50/50 split as closely as 73 cases allow. All points from one simulation stay in one partition, and scalers were fitted on training cases only. The 36 training cases give 4,320 query points; these are not independent simulations. Earlier exploration used all the data, so the 37 test cases were excluded from final fitting but are not a fully blind holdout.

3.2 Model

Each network takes five inputs (temperature, log10 strain rate, density, G, and the query E11) and outputs one Svm value. The architecture is 5–16–16–1 with tanh hidden layers and a linear output (385 parameters per network, 1,925 in total). Five networks with seeds 0–4 are trained with scikit-learn [1] using L-BFGS [2] and L2 penalty α = 0.001, and their predictions are averaged. This is a data-driven surrogate: there is no physics-informed loss term.

3.3 Model Selection

Candidates were compared on training-side evidence only: case-wise cross-validation and a condition holdout (whole temperature/rate groups removed). Table 1 lists the saved results. The query MLP was chosen because it had the best condition-holdout error, which matters most for use beyond the sampled conditions, while its case-CV error was competitive. A PCA + Gaussian-process [3] model was better on case CV but much worse when conditions were held out.

Generalisation to new conditions. Holding out whole temperature/rate groups raises the error to 6.79 (unweighted mean over the nine groups), about a hundred times the random-split error (Figure 2). The hardest group, 200 at 10−5, gives 23.53. Its only available case (60) lies in the training half. The low random-split error therefore mainly shows interpolation between closely related simulated conditions.

Worked example. At temperature 100, rate 10−4, density 2619 and G = 24541, the model predicts 136.20912 at the start of the grid, 99.41858716 at E11 = 0.05 (interpolated) and 75.23194 at the end of the grid.

Reproducibility. A separate refit with the frozen settings reproduced every weight and prediction exactly in 17.26 s with no warnings. Thirteen model/parser/runtime unit tests and three live Model Studio tests pass. This is numerical reproducibility in the same environment, not physical, experimental or cross-platform validation.

5. Discussion

The surrogate is small, fast and reproducible, and closely reproduces the related test curves. Its reliability at other combinations of inputs is not established, even within the numerical ranges, because some groups are sparse and case 60 is irregular. Its main weakness is extrapolation: with 73 cases spread over three temperatures and three rates, some condition groups have very few cases, and errors grow sharply when a whole group is unseen. We propose adding simulations at the sparse and missing conditions (especially 200 and 300) as the first priority. We have not tested this against changing the model size.

The model predicts stress along operational strain only. Turning it into an input for structural analysis needs the solver's stress/strain definitions, confirmed units and the receiving material model. Any ANSYS-format export remains an unvalidated template until those are confirmed. In particular, it must not invent a zero-origin elastic segment or label a total-strain curve as plastic strain.

6. Limitations and Open Questions

  • Units (MPa, °C, s−1) are inferred, not confirmed; the solver, material law and stress/strain definitions are unconfirmed.
  • The 37 test cases were seen during earlier exploration, so the test score is not a fully independent generalisation estimate and may be optimistic.
  • Whether density and modulus levels encode porosity, and the mapping if so, is unverified.
  • There is no agreed CL calculation (flaw geometry, fracture toughness KIC, stress at the critical location), and the material → structure → critical-flaw coupling is undefined.
  • The ANSYS analyses have not been checked against this report's material model; no transfer of predictions into ANSYS has been validated.
  • Stronger generalisation claims need independent experimental data or new simulations.

7. Conclusion

A five-network MLP ensemble reproduces the simulated AlSi10Mg stress response with a mean test RMSE of 0.059 on related conditions and is exactly reproducible. Predictions at other input combinations, even within the simulated temperature, rate, density and modulus ranges, should be treated as unverified. Extending it to new conditions, porosity effects or critical-flaw-length prediction requires the missing definitions and data listed above.
